Privacy is the discipline of governing how information about people is collected, inferred, used, shared, retained, and deleted. It is broader than secrecy: data may remain confidential and still be used for an unexpected or unjustified purpose. Security protects information; privacy determines whether and how processing should occur.

## A Practical Decision Model

Begin with purpose. State the outcome, the people affected, and why the processing is necessary. Then test proportionality: could the outcome be achieved with fewer attributes, less precision, shorter retention, or data that is not identifiable? Finally, make the decision reviewable by recording ownership, safeguards, exceptions, and evidence.

Purpose limitation, minimization, transparency, accountability, individual participation, retention limitation, and privacy by design provide durable lenses for this work. They should shape defaults and architecture, not appear only as a final compliance check.

## Context Changes Risk

Privacy depends on context. The same attribute can be ordinary in one workflow and highly revealing in another. Combining datasets can expose relationships that neither source revealed alone. A new recipient, model, jurisdiction, or retention period can also turn an accepted use into a materially different one.

Teams should therefore reassess privacy when data crosses a boundary or its purpose changes. Consent can support meaningful choice in appropriate contexts, but it does not make excessive collection or harmful processing acceptable by itself.

## From Principle to Practice

Operational privacy connects declared purposes to inventories, classifications, access decisions, retention schedules, de-identification, notices, review processes, and deletion. No single control is sufficient. Effective privacy is a continuing decision process that remains visible as data and systems evolve.

## Summary

Privacy asks what responsible processing means for the people represented in data. Clear purposes, proportionate data use, understandable behavior, accountable ownership, and lifecycle controls turn that question into an engineering and governance practice.
