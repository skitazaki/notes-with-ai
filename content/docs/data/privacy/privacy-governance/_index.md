Privacy governance is the operating model for making consistent, accountable decisions about information relating to people. It connects principles and obligations to ownership, policies, technical controls, review, and evidence across decentralized systems and teams.

## Decision Rights and Roles

Every material processing activity needs an accountable owner. Product and data owners justify purposes and outcomes; engineering teams implement controls; security teams protect systems; privacy and legal specialists interpret requirements and advise on risk; stewards maintain metadata and lifecycle rules. Committees can coordinate difficult decisions but should not obscure who accepts the outcome.

Define which activities are pre-approved, which require assessment, who can approve exceptions, and when escalation is mandatory. High-impact processing, new uses, sensitive populations, external sharing, and automated decisions usually deserve stronger review.

## Policy to Evidence

Policies should map to standards and enforceable controls. A retention policy needs schedules, deletion behavior, accountable systems, and exception handling. A purpose limitation policy needs recorded purposes, use decisions, and controls at access or sharing points.

Maintain inventories, processing records, assessments, decisions, notices, contracts, control results, incidents, and remediation. Evidence should show that the operating model works, not merely that documents exist.

## Continuous Oversight

Measure coverage of inventories and classifications, overdue reviews, exception age, deletion completion, request handling, incidents, and control effectiveness. Review the model when systems, laws, business purposes, or risk change. Use incidents and near misses to improve standards and defaults.

## Summary

Privacy governance makes responsibility durable. Clear ownership, proportionate review, enforceable policy, and trustworthy evidence allow privacy decisions to remain consistent as data moves across teams and platforms.
