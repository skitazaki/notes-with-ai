# Image Generation Prompt — Core Organizing Lenses

Create a publication-quality conceptual diagram for the **Core Organizing Lenses** of the **Artificial Intelligence** documentation.

Title:

"Lenses for Artificial Intelligence"

Subtitle: Six overlapping perspectives for navigating the AI landscape.

Purpose:

Help readers understand that the AI section is organized by a small number of stable conceptual lenses rather than by vendors, products, or hype cycles.
The image should show that these lenses are distinct but closely related, because real AI systems span several concerns at the same time.

Audience:

- Technology leaders and enterprise architects
- AI engineers, platform engineers, data engineers, and senior software engineers
- Readers who need a durable mental map of the AI landscape before going deeper into child pages

Core message:

AI is best navigated through a set of organizing lenses.
Each lens answers a different kind of question, but no production AI system belongs to only one lens.
The diagram should make clear that the documentation section is structured to support cross-navigation across these perspectives.

Required concepts:

Represent all six lenses with these exact labels and supporting questions:

- **Foundations** — What kind of field is AI, and how did it develop?
- **Learning and Models** — How do systems learn and represent capabilities?
- **Data and Context** — What information powers or constrains the system?
- **Engineering and Operations** — How is the system built, evaluated, deployed, and improved?
- **Governance and Risk** — How is the system constrained, reviewed, and made trustworthy?
- **Applications and Platforms** — How is value delivered inside real organizations?

Composition:

- Use a clear central element labeled **Artificial Intelligence**.
- Add a short secondary label in the center: **A navigation map for a broad AI ecosystem**.
- Arrange the six lenses around the center in a balanced hexagonal or circular layout.
- Every lens must have equal visual weight. Do not imply a primary-vs-secondary hierarchy.
- Keep each lens visually separate and clearly bounded.
- Do not number the lenses or present them as steps in a sequence.
- Include a tag group inside each lens to hint at representative topics without adding paragraphs:
  - Foundations: AI vs. ML, symbolic methods, neural networks
  - Learning and Models: supervised learning, deep learning, transformers
  - Data and Context: datasets/medatadata, embeddings/retrieval, memory
  - Engineering and Operations: APIs, evaluation, MLOps, observability, harness
  - Governance and Risk: security, privacy, compliance, AI safety
  - Applications and Platforms: search/automation, chat/agents, enterprise AI

Visual hierarchy:

- The center and six lens labels must be readable at documentation-column width.
- Supporting questions should be short, secondary, and readable.
- Example topic tags should remain concise and scannable.
- Use simple icons only as secondary cues: compass or book for foundations, nodes or network for learning/models, database or layered documents for data/context, pipeline for engineering/operations, shield or scale for governance/risk, and connected blocks for applications/platforms.

Style:

- Clean editorial infographic for technical documentation
- Crisp vector-style shapes and restrained geometry
- Light background with high-contrast, accessible typography
- Professional palette with six coordinated but distinct lens colors and a calm neutral center
- Flat or very subtle depth; no glossy 3D rendering
- Vendor-neutral, timeless, and concept-first
- 16:9 landscape aspect ratio

Do:

- Make the six lenses feel like a navigation framework rather than a system architecture diagram
- Keep the lenses unnumbered and non-sequential
- Keep relationships simple and avoid visual density
- Preserve balanced spacing so the layout feels calm and intentional
- Emphasize that the section structure is stable even if specific technologies change
- Keep the figure readable at article width

Do not:

- Use vendor logos, product UIs, cloud badges, or model-brand names
- Turn the figure into a flowchart, maturity model, org chart, or layered deployment architecture
- Add numeric badges, ordered steps, or ranking cues to the lenses
- Suggest that AI can be understood through a single linear path
- Use tiny paragraphs, dense callouts, or decorative futuristic imagery
- Use overlapping circles, intersecting halos, or Venn-diagram-style regions
