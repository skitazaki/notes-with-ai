# Image Generation Prompt — Data Privacy as a Cross-Cutting Concern

Create a publication-quality conceptual diagram for the **Data Privacy** documentation hub.

Purpose:

Show that data privacy is a cross-cutting discipline in a data ecosystem. It has its own primary concern—responsible processing of information about people—while depending on and shaping several adjacent disciplines. The image should make clear that privacy is not interchangeable with security, governance, compliance, data quality, or AI governance.

Audience:

- Technology leaders and architects
- Data platform engineers and governance practitioners
- Senior engineers who understand data systems but may not be privacy specialists

Core message:

Privacy governs whether and how information about people should be processed across the data lifecycle. Security, governance, compliance, data quality, and AI governance each contribute a distinct but connected capability.

Composition:

- Use a centered, clearly dominant circle or rounded panel labeled **Data Privacy**.
- Subtitle the center: **Responsible processing of information about people**.
- Arrange six surrounding, connected panels in a balanced radial layout. Do not use a literal multi-circle Venn diagram; it becomes unreadable and wrongly implies that the disciplines are identical.
- Use subtle overlapping halos, shared boundary arcs, or connector bands between the center and surrounding panels to express interdependence.
- The surrounding panels must have these labels and short descriptors:
  - **Information Security** — Protects against unauthorized access, alteration, and loss
  - **Data Governance** — Establishes ownership, policies, and decision rights
  - **Compliance** — Turns applicable obligations into verifiable requirements
  - **Data Quality** — Supports accurate and fit-for-purpose information
  - **AI Governance** — Oversees responsible AI data use and automated decisions
  - **Data Lifecycle** — Connects collection, use, sharing, retention, and deletion
- Use directional connectors from each surrounding panel to Data Privacy. The connectors should indicate contribution and interaction, not a linear sequence or hierarchy.
- Include a thin outer lifecycle ring or understated perimeter labels: **Collect → Store → Use → Share → Retain → Delete**. This reinforces that privacy applies continuously.

Visual hierarchy:

- Data Privacy is the focal point and should be the largest labeled element.
- The six surrounding disciplines have equal visual weight.
- Keep all text short, large, and readable at documentation-column width.
- Use simple icons only as secondary cues: shield for security, compass or policy document for governance, checklist for compliance, checkmark/data grid for quality, connected nodes for AI governance, and circular arrows for lifecycle.

Style:

- Clean editorial infographic for technical documentation
- Crisp vector-style geometry and restrained line work
- Light background with high contrast, accessible text
- Professional palette of deep blue or teal for privacy, with muted differentiated accent colors for the connected disciplines
- Flat or very subtle depth; no glossy 3D effects
- Vendor-neutral, timeless, and concept-first
- 16:9 landscape aspect ratio

Do:

- Make the distinct role of every discipline legible without implying that privacy owns all of them
- Emphasize that security safeguards data, while privacy governs appropriate processing and use
- Show privacy as active across the entire lifecycle
- Leave comfortable whitespace so the diagram remains calm and scannable

Do not:

- Use vendor logos, regulatory logos, country flags, or jurisdiction-specific symbols
- Depict legal advice, compliance certification, surveillance imagery, locks as the only privacy metaphor, or stock-business people
- Create a dense system architecture, flowchart, dashboard, or data pipeline
- Use a six-way Venn diagram, tiny explanatory paragraphs, or text that cannot be read at article width
- Suggest that privacy is merely a security function or a final compliance check
