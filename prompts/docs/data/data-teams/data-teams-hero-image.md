# Image Generation Prompt — Data Teams Hero Image

## Title

**Data Teams**

**Subtitle**

Organizational patterns for balancing accountability, coordination, governance, and platform enablement.

---

# Purpose

Create a publication-quality conceptual illustration that introduces four common organizational patterns for data teams.

The illustration should communicate that data-team design is an organizational architecture decision rather than a maturity model, technology choice, or reporting structure.

The primary message is:

> Data organizations differ mainly in **who is accountable for data work** and **how that work is coordinated across the enterprise.**

Readers should immediately understand that multiple valid organizational patterns exist, each representing different trade-offs rather than increasing maturity.

---

# Core Design Dimensions

Represent the organizational landscape as a balanced **2 × 2 matrix**.

The two axes describe organizational design decisions instead of "better" or "worse" states.

## Horizontal axis

**Accountability Model**

Left endpoint:

**Central Accountability**

Right endpoint:

**Domain Accountability**

Meaning:

Who ultimately owns the outcomes, priorities, and long-term evolution of data work?

---

## Vertical axis

**Coordination Model**

Bottom endpoint:

**Independent Coordination**

Top endpoint:

**Shared Coordination**

Meaning:

To what extent do teams rely on shared platforms, governance, standards, and reusable capabilities instead of operating independently?

---

# Organizational Patterns

Give all four quadrants equal visual importance.

Do **not** imply progression or maturity.

---

## 1. Centralized

Location:

Lower-left quadrant

Concept:

A central data organization owns nearly all delivery, governance, and prioritization.

Visual cues:

- one prominent central team
- multiple request arrows converging inward
- domains consuming outputs
- little platform abstraction
- direct coordination

Primary message:

One organization delivers data capabilities for everyone.

---

## 2. Embedded

Location:

Lower-right quadrant

Concept:

Each business domain independently owns and delivers its own data work.

Visual cues:

- isolated domain teams
- duplicated capabilities
- minimal shared services
- weak cross-domain connections

Primary message:

Domains optimize locally with limited enterprise coordination.

---

## 3. Platform-enabled Centralization

Location:

Upper-left quadrant

Concept:

A central data organization remains accountable while using shared platform capabilities to improve consistency and scalability.

Visual cues:

- central organization remains visually dominant
- platform services clearly operated by the central team
- bidirectional operational links between central team and domains
- governance integrated into central operations

Primary message:

Platform strengthens centralized delivery rather than replacing it.

---

## 4. Domain Data Products

Location:

Upper-right quadrant

Concept:

Business domains own interoperable data products while consuming a shared self-service platform under federated governance.

This quadrant must look fundamentally different from Platform-enabled Centralization.

Visual cues:

### Domain product network

Show several autonomous domain product nodes.

Examples:

- Sales Product
- Customer Product
- Finance Product
- Supply Product

The products should publish to and consume from one another through lateral connections.

Avoid hub-and-spoke topology.

---

### Federated governance

Instead of placing governance above the domains, depict governance as a lightweight boundary surrounding the entire domain product network.

Represent governance through:

- shared contracts
- policies
- semantic standards
- interoperability rules
- quality guardrails

Governance should protect collaboration rather than centrally directing it.

---

### Shared platform

Place a reusable self-service platform beneath the domain products.

Examples:

- Storage
- Compute
- Catalog
- Identity
- Observability
- Developer Services

The platform enables the domains but does not own them.

Primary message:

Domains own products.

The platform provides leverage.

Governance enables trusted interoperability.

---

# Cross-cutting Elements

Across the entire illustration, include lightweight visual cues for:

- governance
- reusable platform capabilities
- business domains
- data products
- request flows
- service interfaces

If AI appears at all, represent it only as a subtle capability spanning all quadrants.

Never depict AI as a fifth organizational model.

---

# Visual Hierarchy

Allocate visual attention approximately as follows:

- 80% organizational patterns
- 15% axes and labels
- 5% supporting annotations

Avoid large explanatory text inside the figure.

The illustration should be understandable within five seconds.

---

# Style

- editorial technical infographic
- publication quality
- clean vector illustration
- modern documentation aesthetic
- light background
- restrained enterprise color palette (4–6 colors)
- subtle shadows only if necessary
- generous whitespace
- consistent iconography
- vendor neutral
- no product branding
- no logos
- no office people
- no photorealism

---

# Composition

Use a perfectly balanced 2 × 2 matrix.

Keep all four quadrants visually equal.

Do not emphasize any quadrant through size, brightness, or decorative effects.

Axes should appear structural rather than analytical.

---

# Caption

> Four organizational patterns showing how accountability and coordination shape data organizations.

---

# Do

- emphasize organizational relationships
- distinguish patterns through structure instead of color
- make accountability immediately understandable
- make coordination immediately understandable
- show governance as an enabling constraint
- show the platform as shared infrastructure rather than centralized ownership
- make Domain Data Products visibly interconnected

---

# Do Not

- create a maturity model
- imply organizational evolution
- use sequential arrows
- show one "best" pattern
- make Platform-enabled Centralization and Domain Data Products look similar
- draw enterprise architecture diagrams
- overload the figure with labels
- depict AI as the destination
- use vendor logos or cloud-provider branding
