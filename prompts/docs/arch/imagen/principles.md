# Image Generation Prompt - Architecture Principles

Create a publication-quality conceptual process diagram titled:

"Architecture Principles"

Subtitle: From goals to durable decision guidance.

Purpose:

Show how business and engineering goals become architecture principles, and then how those principles influence a decision framework, an architecture decision, and the resulting views and implementation.

Style:

- clean technical infographic
- vector-style editorial layout
- light background
- professional documentation aesthetic
- crisp, readable labels
- 16:9 aspect ratio
- vendor-neutral and concept-first
- no product branding or logos

Composition:

Create a clear left-to-right process flow with five stages connected by arrows:

1. Business and engineering goals
2. Architecture principles
3. Decision framework
4. Architecture decision
5. Views and implementation

Use rounded rectangular panels or soft connected nodes, with the middle stage, "Architecture principles," visually emphasized as the core translation point between strategic intent and practical decision-making.
The flow should be easy to read at a glance and should feel like a reasoning chain, not a deployment diagram or technology topology.

Add small supporting labels beneath or inside each stage so the conceptual logic is explicit:

- Goals: strategic intent, priorities, and constraints
- Principles: durable preference and default guidance
- Decision framework: tradeoff analysis, evidence, and exceptions
- Architecture decision: a concrete choice in context
- Views and implementation: communication and execution of the decision

The diagram should communicate a steady progression where goals create reusable architectural guidance, and that guidance shapes how teams evaluate alternatives and make decisions repeatedly.

Central message:

Architecture principles turn intent into durable guidance.
They do not replace a decision, but they shape the default, the burden of proof, and the consistency of many decisions over time.

Do:

- keep the visual hierarchy clear and balanced
- make the reasoning chain easy to follow
- reinforce the idea that principles are reusable guidance across many decisions
- use a modern, technical-docs aesthetic
- keep labels concise and precise

Do not:

- do not make this a deployment diagram or system architecture map
- do not add product logos, icons, or vendor branding
- do not overload the figure with small text
- do not imply that principles are absolute laws or single technology choices
- do not treat the diagram as a generic workflow without architectural meaning

Audience:

- software architects
- engineering leaders
- platform and system designers
- technical teams making recurring design decisions
